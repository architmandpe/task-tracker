Privacy Policy

Last updated: 1 August 2026

Cadence is a personal task-tracking project. This policy explains, in plain terms, what it stores and who else sees it.

What is collected

  • Your email address — either typed at sign-up, or supplied by Google if you sign in with Google.
  • A password hash, if you registered with a password. The password itself is never stored.
  • Your Google account identifier, if you sign in with Google, so the account can be recognised on your return.
  • Your tasks — titles, descriptions, due dates, priorities, status and recurrence.
  • A record of actions the assistant takes on your behalf, so you can review what changed.

There is no analytics, advertising, tracking pixel, or third-party cookie. The only cookie set is the one that keeps you signed in.

Who else sees your data

Using the assistant sends the text of your tasks to third-party services. This is how the feature works, and it is worth knowing:

  • Groq — receives your message and relevant task text in order to generate the assistant's reply.
  • Google (Generative AI embeddings) — receives task text to build the search index that lets the assistant find tasks by meaning.
  • Render — hosts the application.
  • Neon — hosts the database where your account and tasks are stored.

Your data is not sold, and is not shared with anyone beyond the services above.

Signing in with Google

Cadence requests only your basic profile: openid, email and profile. It cannot read your Gmail, Drive, Calendar or contacts, and it never sees your Google password. If an account already exists under the same verified email address, signing in with Google attaches to that same account rather than creating a second one.

Retention and deletion

Your account and tasks are kept until you ask for them to be removed. Deleting a task hides it and keeps it briefly so the action can be undone. To have your account and all associated data deleted, or to request a copy of it, email the address below.

Security

Traffic is served over HTTPS. Sessions use a signed, httpOnly cookie that JavaScript cannot read. Passwords are stored only as bcrypt hashes. That said, this is a personal project rather than a commercial service — please do not store sensitive or confidential information in it.
